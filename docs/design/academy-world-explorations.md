# Academy World Explorations

**Status:** Exploratory — nothing in this document is canon until explicitly approved
**Type:** Worldbuilding and campus-design workbench
**Started:** 2026-08-24

## How to Use This Document

This file holds possibilities, questions, names, layouts, and story seeds. It
may contain conflicting ideas. Approved decisions are promoted to
[Academy World Definition](academy-world-definition.md); rejected ideas may be
deleted or retained here with a short rejection note when the history is useful.

## Wider-World Story Seed: Merlin and Nilrem

At some point in the past, Headmaster Merlin descended into the deepest depths
of an as-yet-unnamed place. It is known as one of the most dangerous places on
an as-yet-undefined world, continent, region, or plane. Merlin is one of the
only people known to have returned alive from its depths.

He did not return alone.

Something in that descent created **Nilrem**, a dark copy or inversion of
Merlin. Nilrem now haunts the world as a consequence of what happened there.

### Deliberately Unanswered

- What is the dangerous place, and what does “deepest depths” literally mean?
- Why did Merlin enter it?
- Was Nilrem copied from Merlin, separated from him, shaped from something he
  left behind, or created by an outside intelligence?
- Did Merlin knowingly bring Nilrem back?
- Does the public know Nilrem exists?
- Is Nilrem physically independent, intermittently present, or tied to Merlin?
- What does Nilrem want?
- Does the Academy predate the descent, result from it, or merely inherit its
  consequences?
- Does this story affect the campus physically, or should it remain part of the
  wider world until later?

No answer is implied by the reversed name. The premise is preserved without
turning it into accepted lore.

## Campus Hub Goals

The hub should be understandable in a few seconds but feel like a functioning
school rather than four quest stations around a menu plaza.

Useful qualities to explore:

- one obvious everyday circulation route;
- a strong central social or orientation space;
- the Campus Shop placed where ordinary student traffic supports it;
- four memorable elemental teaching anchors that feel actively used;
- space for recurring students, staff, notices, and small events;
- at least one visible route outward so the campus does not feel sealed off from
  the world;
- reserved edges or thresholds where elemental biomes can connect later;
- restricted or hidden routes that contrast with the welcoming public campus;
- convenient Arena access without making competitive play the Academy's center.

## Hub Organization Models

### Model A: Four Courts Around a Central Commons

The Campus Shop and social commons occupy the center. Four elemental teaching
courts sit on the outer sides, with the Arena and outward travel beyond them.

**Strengths:** Immediately readable; short travel; each teacher is easy to find.

**Risks:** Can feel like a symmetrical game board or elemental theme park. The
world may look designed around UI categories rather than history and terrain.

### Model B: Main Walk and Academic Loop

Players arrive on a busy main walk containing the Campus Shop and commons. A
loop branches through older academic grounds, passing the four teaching places
in an irregular order before reconnecting near the Arena or departure point.

**Strengths:** Feels inhabited and grown over time; naturally creates foot
traffic; supports shortcuts and discoveries.

**Risks:** Needs strong landmarks so the loop remains legible. One teacher can
feel inconvenient if spacing is careless.

### Model C: Terraced Campus

The campus rises through two or three compact levels. Public and commercial
life sits near arrival; teaching places occupy middle terraces; authority,
advanced study, or outward routes sit higher or farther back.

**Strengths:** Strong views and hierarchy; architecture can express status;
different teaching places can respond to elevation and terrain.

**Risks:** More complex traversal and camera composition; vertical status may
accidentally imply an elemental hierarchy.

### Hybrid Candidate: Commons Plus Irregular Teaching Loop

A compact public commons acts as the player's anchor. The Shop faces the main
pedestrian route rather than occupying a ceremonial center. An irregular loop
connects the four teaching places, with small cross-paths returning to the
commons. The Arena sits on one accessible edge. One or more outward paths are
reserved for later biome connections.

This combines the clarity of Model A with the lived-in quality of Model B. It is
a candidate, not a recommendation that has been accepted.

## Campus Shop Placement Ideas

### Near the Arrival Route

The Merriweathers catch new arrivals, deliveries, and returning excursion
traffic. This makes commercial logistics believable and keeps purchases
convenient.

### Facing the Central Commons

The shop becomes part of daily campus life and a natural information exchange.
It risks making commerce too visually central unless its scale stays modest.

### At a Crossroads Between Public and Academic Areas

The shop serves students moving between classes while remaining distinct from
ceremonial or administrative space. This offers the strongest everyday logic.

## Elemental Teaching-Place Ideas

These are functional and spatial prompts, not accepted names or architecture.

### Fire

- An open masonry yard where heat, smoke, and mistakes can vent safely.
- A workshop court shared with magical craftspeople.
- A stepped demonstration pit with fireproof observation ledges.
- Located downwind or beyond a firebreak rather than arbitrarily placed east.
- Could later connect to volcanic, geothermal, ashland, or forge-like biome
  content without putting that entire biome on campus.

### Water

- A teaching court built around a managed canal, reservoir, spring, or cistern.
- A covered rain court whose channels reveal water control during lessons.
- A lakeside or riverside pavilion if the Academy geography supports natural
  water.
- Could later connect to coast, wetland, river, flooded ruin, or deep-water
  biome content.

### Earth

- A stoneworking yard, terraced garden, exposed foundation, or small teaching
  quarry.
- A low amphitheater where the ground itself is altered and reset between
  classes.
- Positioned near real geology or heavy campus construction rather than given a
  decorative pile of rocks.
- Could later connect to mountains, caverns, badlands, forests, or ancient
  buried structures.

### Wind

- An open elevated terrace, bridge court, roof garden, or tower base.
- A place with flags, chimes, suspended instruments, or moving practice targets
  that make invisible air movement legible.
- It needs exposure and clear space, not necessarily the highest point on the
  map.
- Could later connect to highlands, cliffs, storm country, floating terrain, or
  upper-atmosphere content.

## Relationship Between Teaching Places and Biomes

Defer the exact solution, but preserve these integration models for later
comparison:

### Campus Thresholds

Each teaching place sits near a route that eventually leads toward its element's
field biome. The classroom remains on campus; advanced instruction moves into
the world.

### Shared Expedition Departure

All biomes are reached from one neutral departure system. Teaching places do not
need literal adjacency, but their professors direct students outward through
quests.

### Local Demonstration Pockets

Each teaching place contains a small, controlled environmental sample. These
are teaching aids, not full biomes. Full elemental regions exist elsewhere.

### Uneven Geography

Some elements have natural campus adjacency while others require travel. This
can make the Academy feel located in a real place rather than perfectly centered
between four equal biomes.

## Other Hub Places Worth Testing

These are candidate functions, not a required building list:

- a public commons where students gather between classes;
- an arrival court, gate, dock, bridge, station, or portal terminus;
- an authority space associated with Merlin and Academy governance;
- a neutral practice field for cross-element instruction;
- a library, archive, observatory, or repository used for research and quests
  without duplicating the Spellbook UI;
- a dining hall, refectory, courtyard kitchen, or market-like social space;
- residences that establish daily life even if most rooms are not enterable;
- creature care, summoning safety, or containment facilities;
- maintenance, delivery, storage, and staff spaces that make the institution
  physically credible;
- an old or restricted campus edge that can conceal the underground route;
- an outward-facing expedition or receiving yard;
- small clubs, student societies, memorials, gardens, and notice boards that can
  host rotating character interactions.

## Questions for the Hub Pass

1. What is the player's most common repeated route through campus?
2. What place functions as the social heart, if any?
3. Should the four elemental teachers feel like members of one institution or
   stewards of four distinct traditions housed together?
4. Are classes mostly public demonstrations, small seminars, dangerous
   practical work, or a mix that differs by teacher?
5. Which places remain lively after their associated UI or quest is complete?
6. What campus edge suggests the wider world most strongly?
7. Which spatial relationship communicates the Academy's welcoming face and
   which reveals its harsher reality?
8. Which buildings are visible but not enterable, and what essential campus
   functions do they explain?
