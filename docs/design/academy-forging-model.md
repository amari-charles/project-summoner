# Academy Forging Model

**Status:** Accepted direction
**Date:** 2026-05-03
**Current model:** Magic academy curriculum with semesters, Enrollments, course arcs, transcript identity, and graduation into online PvP

## Core Premise

Fateforged is about forging a summoner through permanent PvE choices, then taking that summoner into online battles. PvP is not meant to be perfectly equalized. A stronger or weaker campaign result can carry into online strength, as long as the consequences are intentional, legible, and earned.

## Academy Classes / Parallel Tracks

The player progresses through an academy curriculum rather than a geographical route. Each semester offers limited Enrollments, and classes belong to parallel subject tracks.

## Settled Core Model

- The structure uses four academy arcs/years.
- Each year has two semesters, for eight semesters total.
- Each semester gives the player a limited number of **Enrollments**.
- One Enrollment commits the player to one class/course arc.
- Classes are usually multi-activity course arcs, not single fights.
- Classes belong to parallel tracks.
- Classes can define prerequisites.
- Honors is an enrollment mode or class variant that requires prior eligibility.
- Honors does not automatically cost extra Enrollments.
- Eligibility for Honors is track-specific by default.
- Class requirements can vary by class: specific prerequisites, Honors in a prior class, multiple completed classes, cross-track combinations, or other explicit conditions.
- Completing a class grants exclusive reward choices: the player chooses one reward and permanently loses access to the others.
- The summoner's final PvP identity is shaped by classes taken, classes skipped, Honors eligibility earned, Honors classes completed, and reward choices made.

## Semester / Class Structure

The academy is paced through semesters. A semester gives the player a fixed Enrollment budget, and the player enrolls sequentially rather than planning the entire semester upfront.

Class model:

- A class is a course arc.
- A course arc usually contains multiple activities: lessons, practice battles, reward choices, labs, duels, or a final class exam.
- Most classes should include combat, but a class does not have to be only combat.
- Enrolling in a class should feel like a meaningful commitment, not a renamed single map node.
- Class cost can vary by class, but the main scarcity is the semester's limited Enrollments.

Default class rhythm:

> Introduce the idea, let the player practice it, pressure-test it, then conclude with a grade and reward.

This is a soft template, not a fixed formula. Classes should be defined individually as their mechanics, track, and narrative role require.

## Grading / Honors Principle

Grades should mostly measure how well the player handled a class, not whether the summoner is allowed to continue existing. Graduation should be generous; excellence should be differentiating.

- **Pass:** Complete the class and receive the normal reward.
- **Honors eligibility:** Earned by meeting class-specific performance objectives.
- **High grades:** Matter for transcript identity, status, and stricter future prerequisites.
- **Failure or poor performance:** Usually means a worse grade, missed Honors eligibility, or weaker reward access rather than a dead campaign.

This keeps permanence meaningful while reducing the chance that early balance mistakes permanently ruin a summoner for reasons the player could not reasonably understand.

## Performance Objective Principle

Performance objectives should be authored per class, not mechanically dictated by track. Tracks can suggest the flavor of excellence, but the class itself decides what strong performance means.

Good objective vectors include:

- winning under pressure
- preserving health or units
- using limited resources efficiently
- completing a tactical condition
- beating an unusual enemy setup
- winning with a restricted toolset
- protecting something
- ending with board advantage
- recovering from disadvantage
- meeting a class-specific challenge

Honors should mean the player proved they understood that class, not merely that they won harder.

## Practice / Assessment Principle

Classes can contain repeatable practice activities, but official assessments are permanent.

- Practice activities may be repeatable so players can learn, experiment, and stabilize before consequence locks in.
- Final exams, grade checks, Honors checks, and exclusive reward choices are not repeatable by default.
- The transcript records official assessment outcomes, not every practice attempt.
- This allows learning without making grades and rewards feel reversible.

## Reward Preview Principle

Classes should show reward expectations before enrollment, but the exact reward structure can vary by class.

Possible structures:

- **Fixed preview:** The catalog shows the specific reward earned for completing the class.
- **Choice preview:** The catalog shows a fixed set of rewards and the player chooses one.
- **Pool preview:** The catalog shows the reward pool or category the class draws from.
- **Conditional preview:** The catalog shows how grades, Honors, or special objectives can improve the reward options.

The player should understand what kind of permanent value they are spending an Enrollment on, even when the exact final choice depends on performance or a reward pool.

## Transcript Identity Principle

There are no formal majors or minors for now. The summoner's identity emerges from the transcript: classes completed, tracks studied, Honors marks earned, grades, assessment outcomes, and permanent rewards chosen.

The game may derive readable labels or titles from the transcript later, but those labels should describe the build rather than constrain progression.

## Enrollment Budget Principle

The exact Enrollment count is not fixed yet. It should be tuned after class scope and cost range are clearer.

Target feel:

- Each semester should give enough Enrollments for multiple real choices.
- Each year should let the player build a direction without completing everything.
- Across eight semesters, the player should graduate with a rich but incomplete transcript.
- Class costs can vary, so the semester budget and class cost range must be tuned together.

## Class Cost Principle

Most classes should cost **1 Enrollment**. Larger, rarer, or unusually valuable classes can cost **2+ Enrollments** when the increased commitment is clear before enrollment.

- Honors does not automatically cost extra.
- Higher cost should represent broader scope, stronger opportunity, or unusual access, not just a hidden tax on good options.
- Class cost must be previewed clearly in the catalog.

## Campus Shop Principle

The old merchant/caravan role becomes a campus shop or equivalent academy service. It is part of campus life rather than a campaign-map stop.

- The shop can be accessed at any time from the Academy Hub.
- The offering can change by semester.
- The shop can sell or provide support goods such as equipment, consumables, common cards, upgrade services, or tutoring-like utility.
- Special variants can exist as events: visiting vendors, department quartermasters, alumni traders, or forbidden/black-market sources.
- Shop exclusives are allowed.
- The shop can sell any reward type when appropriate: cards, traits/upgrades, equipment, services, consumables, rare items, or exclusive items.
- Shop power should be controlled through price, rotation, prerequisites, stock limits, and access gates rather than by banning whole categories.

The shop preserves the useful merchant function while fitting the academy structure.

## Economy Principle

Enrollments and gold serve different purposes.

- **Enrollments** represent time and attention. They are used for classes.
- **Gold/credits** represent spending power. They are used for the Campus Shop and related services.
- **Grades, Honors, and transcript state** represent access/status. They can unlock classes, shop tiers, special offers, or event opportunities.

Gold should come from official progression, not repeatable grinding:

- official battle or assessment completion rewards gold once
- better performance can add a bounded bonus
- semester starts can grant a small allowance
- events can occasionally grant extra gold
- repeatable practice activities should not generate gold

Profession-style classes are not part of the core model for now. They can return later as optional class themes or support paths if the economy needs more depth.

## Events / Notices Principle

Events appear through the Academy Hub as notices or temporary opportunities rather than campaign-map nodes.

- Events can appear between classes, at semester start/end, or when triggered by transcript state, grades, rivals, shop access, equipment, or specific classes.
- Events can be free, gold-costing, or rarely Enrollment-costing.
- Events can provide flavor, social/rival moments, shop opportunities, challenge battles, upgrade services, or small reward choices.
- Major power events are allowed but should be rare, clearly framed, and intentionally gated.

The Course Catalog remains the main progression engine. Events add texture and opportunity without becoming a second campaign map.

## Academy Hub Visual Reference

The Academy Hub should feel like a game hub map, not an app dashboard. Use a campus/location-map presentation where buildings or areas represent major systems.

Current visual reference set:

- Game UI Database hub/map examples: https://www.gameuidatabase.com/index.php?scrn=6&scroll=150

Useful inspiration directions:

- town/base hubs where buildings are game systems
- academy/campus maps with labeled facilities
- destination screens that imply place, not generic navigation

The first-pass campus can be placeholder art, but the target is a readable academy map with destinations such as Class Hall, Dorms, Campus Shop, Mission Hall, and Arena Gate. Completed class history can live inside Class Hall rather than as a separate Transcript Office destination.

## Graduation Capstone Principle

After Year 4, the summoner faces a **Graduation Capstone**. This is the campaign final exam, not a class final.

- The capstone tests the whole summoner: deck, traits, equipment, class choices, strengths, and weaknesses.
- Passing the capstone graduates the summoner into online PvP.
- Capstone performance can affect final transcript status, title, reward, or prestige.
- The capstone should give the campaign a real ending without casually invalidating the entire journey.

## Loss / Failure Principle

Failure should matter, but the game should avoid letting one unclear or badly balanced loss casually ruin a permanent PvP summoner.

- Practice losses are retryable learning.
- Official assessment losses can produce poor grades, missed Honors, weaker reward access, or transcript marks.
- Graduation Capstone losses should probably allow retries, while first-clear performance can still affect prestige, title, final reward, or transcript status.
- Failure should usually remove upside rather than delete the character or invalidate the campaign.

## PvP Readability Principle

Online opponents do not need advance knowledge of a summoner's campaign transcript before the match starts. PvP can preserve mystery and discovery.

- The player should clearly understand their own summoner, rewards, traits, equipment, and transcript.
- Opponents learn the summoner through play.
- Traits, equipment, unusual card behavior, and status effects should be understandable when they appear.
- Combat log, tooltips, reveal timing, and post-match details can provide clarity without requiring pre-match scouting.

## Track Design Principle

Tracks should create meaningfully different PvP summoners, not just themed piles of upgrades. Each track should define a build axis with clear strengths, weaknesses, reward types, and opponent-readable identity.

A track should answer:

- What kind of power does this summoner specialize in?
- What kind of player decisions does this track reward?
- What weakness does choosing this track leave?
- How does this identity become understandable through play?
- What permanent rewards does this track own: cards, traits, evolutions, commands, mechanics, or items?

Useful track template:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Core fantasy | What the track feels like |
| Mechanical domain | What systems it upgrades |
| Skill rewarded | What player behavior it favors |
| PvP identity | How opponents experience it |
| Weakness | What it does not solve |
| Reward types | What permanent rewards it grants |

## Online Reward Principle

Online play can grant real combat rewards, but those rewards should not replace the Academy as the main forge. The current preferred rule is a strict **online reward allowance**: a summoner may equip only a small number of online-earned combat rewards in PvP, starting with one total online reward.

Examples:

- one online-earned card
- one online-earned card upgrade
- one online-earned weapon/relic

This lets online rewards matter while keeping the campaign transcript, class choices, and Enrollments as the dominant source of summoner identity. Later PvP formats can vary the allowance, such as Academy Pure (`0`), Standard (`1`), or Open (`3`).

## Upgrade / Trait Principle

Traits are **chosen permanent modifiers**. A trait can be flavorful, mechanical, or purely stat-based; the important distinction is that the player chose it and it permanently changes a card or summoner.

Trait owners:

- **Card traits:** chosen modifiers attached to a specific card.
- **Summoner traits:** chosen modifiers attached to the summoner/build globally.

Examples:

- `Fire Wisp` gains +1 summoned unit.
- `Mana Bolt` chains once.
- `Pebbloom` gains +10% HP.
- The summoner's first spell each battle costs less.
- Fire-affinity damage increases.

Not every upgrade is a trait. Non-trait progression includes:

- automatic level stat growth
- trait slot/capacity unlocks
- new card acquisition
- equipment acquisition
- access to additional cards, copies, or consistency effects
- class eligibility, Honors marks, and transcript state
- currency, cosmetics, titles, and status rewards

Design rule:

> Automatic growth is not a trait. Chosen permanent modifiers are traits, even when the modifier is a simple stat increase.

This keeps UI and progression readable: card level stats are shown as baseline growth, while chosen card traits appear as named permanent badges/nodes on the card.

## Reward Vectors

Class rewards can use several reward vectors. Rarity and specialness are not tied to the vector itself; any vector can appear as a minor early reward or as a major late/Honors reward.

Core reward vectors:

| Vector | Meaning |
|---|---|
| New cards | Summon or spell cards added to the summoner's permanent deck pool |
| Card traits | Chosen permanent modifiers attached to a specific card |
| Summoner traits | Chosen permanent global modifiers attached to the summoner/build |
| Equipment | Gear, relics, weapons, or items with slot/equip rules |
| Consistency tools | Effects that improve reliability, such as extra copies, opening-hand rules, tutoring, or filtering |
| Transcript / eligibility | Honors marks, class unlocks, thesis access, or future prerequisite state |

Scaling principle:

> Later-year, deeper-track, and Honors rewards should be more meaningful versions of the same reward vectors, not separate reward categories.

Examples:

- New card: basic summon -> rare signature summon.
- Card trait: small stat boost -> behavior-changing evolution.
- Summoner trait: minor bonus -> build-defining doctrine.
- Equipment: small item -> signature relic.
- Consistency tool: extra copy -> major opening-hand or tutoring rule.
- Eligibility: class unlock -> thesis/capstone access.

## Deck Editing Principle

Players can freely edit their deck using any cards and tools they own. The permanent constraint is what the summoner has earned, not whether the player is allowed to arrange those earned pieces.

Rewards can still improve deck quality by adding new cards, additional copies, upgrades, traits, equipment, tutoring effects, filtering effects, or other build resources. The reward is the owned tool, not permission to edit the deck.

## MVP Spine

The first playable academy campaign should focus on the core loop rather than every possible adjacent system.

- 4 years, 2 semesters each.
- Fixed Enrollments per semester.
- Course Catalog with class choices.
- Classes as small course arcs.
- Grades and Honors outcomes.
- Rewards from cards, traits, equipment, consistency tools, and eligibility.
- Campus Shop with rotating stock and gold from official progression.
- Graduation Capstone after Year 4.
- Graduated summoner enters online PvP.

Do not prioritize formal majors, profession systems, remediation systems, pre-match opponent scouting, or complex event webs for the first version.

## Year 1 First Pass

Year 1 should be guided enough to teach the game, but not choice-free. By the end of Semester 1, every player should have at least one summon and one spell.

### Semester 1

Required:

- **Introduction to Magic 101:** teaches core battle flow and grants one neutral/basic summon plus one neutral/basic spell.

Required foundation choice:

- **Summoning Basics:** early unit/conjuration fundamentals; rewards a summon.
- **Practical Spellcraft:** early spellcasting fundamentals; rewards a spell.

Element elective:

- **Intro to Fire**
- **Intro to Water**
- **Intro to Earth**
- **Intro to Air**

Year 1 intro element classes should grant one elemental summon and one elemental spell. They teach the element through concrete cards, not abstract affinity or trait systems yet.

Semester 1 expected outcome:

- 1 neutral/basic summon
- 1 neutral/basic spell
- 1 additional summon or spell from the foundation choice
- 1 elemental summon
- 1 elemental spell

This gives every player at least two summons, at least two spells, and one extra card based on their first foundation choice.

### Semester 2

Semester 2 uses the broader Course Catalog rather than narrow choice buckets. Available classes can include:

- **Foundations of Magic II:** continuation of the first required foundation course.
- **Introduction to Empowerment:** introduces chosen upgrades/traits.
- **Introduction to Mana Channeling:** teaches channeling more mana through a summon card, allowing larger summons or more units called at once.
- Any untaken **Intro Element** class.
- Follow-up classes for the element taken in Semester 1.

## First-Pass Track Set

Track names can still change. The important decision is that tracks are build philosophies, not exclusive card-type silos. Any track can offer cards, traits, equipment, consistency tools, or eligibility when appropriate.

| Track | Build Meaning | Typical Reward Bias |
|---|---|
| Affinity | Element identity and elemental synergy | elemental cards, elemental traits, element-specific card traits |
| Binding | Creature/summon depth and companion evolution | summon cards, card traits for units, signature creature upgrades |
| Arcana | Spell effects, mana, and magical field control | spell cards, spell traits, mana traits, magical equipment |
| Warding | Defense, sustain, survival, anti-burst | defensive traits, protective cards, shields, relics |
| Warfare | Tempo, aggression, dueling, PvP pressure | burst traits, fast cards, finisher tools, pressure equipment |
| Command | Consistency, formations, targeting behavior, deck control | consistency tools, behavior traits, formation tools, command-flavored cards |

Each year, the player can only take some classes. Taking `Spellcraft I` may unlock `Spellcraft II`; skipping it can close or delay that branch. This makes the summoner feel like a product of what they studied.

## Why This Differs From The Campaign Map

Old map question:

> Which route did I take?

Academy curriculum question:

> What did I choose to master?

Substantive differences:

- Choices map directly to identity: Spellcraft, Warding, Dueling, Summoning.
- Opportunity cost is clearer: limited Enrollments explain why you cannot do everything.
- Progression has depth: early classes unlock advanced classes and thesis options.
- Performance can matter locally: Pass / Honors / Distinction per class.
- Online identity emerges through play: traits, equipment, unusual card behavior, and status effects become readable when they appear.

## Why This Is Promising

- Strongly matches the magic academy theme.
- Makes permanence feel like education, not arbitrary route locking.
- Supports PvP asymmetry without needing perfect fairness.
- Lets failure matter through missed Honors or lower grades, while still allowing eventual graduation.
- Gives a natural UI metaphor: course catalog, semester schedule, transcript, campus shop, and graduation capstone.
